01The one idea

The system never asks what should this look like? It asks what is this? — and the look follows from the answer.

That sounds like a small difference. It isn't. “What should this look like” has no correct answer, so everyone who touches the work gives a different one and the deck drifts. “What is this?” has one answer, and the answer picks the component.

So everything goes down the same ladder. Six rungs, always in this order.

  1. What does the reader need? The question they arrived with.
  2. Where is it going? A deck, a web page, a report, a spreadsheet, a poster.
  3. What is it, structurally? A heading, a claim, a piece of evidence, an aside.
  4. Can it be read? Contrast, size, measure, order, labelling.
  5. Does it sound like us? Colour, type, the accent, the ground.
  6. Anything left over? Flourish.

Higher wins. When brand expression and reading clarity want different things, clarity wins — every time, without a conversation. That is what the order is for: it settles arguments before they start, so nobody has to win them on taste or seniority.

And the ladder can stop. If something fails at rung three it never reaches rung five. A chart that cannot be made honest does not get a colour scheme. You will see that happen to your own numbers further down.

How much freedom you have: three tiers

Fixed

Uncertainty. There is a right answer. Find it and apply it. Contrast ratios, heading structure, the spacing scale, what an edge treatment means. The system enforces these and fails the build if they break.

Adaptable

Ambiguity. No single correct answer. Which sentence gets the breakout, how often you change ground, whether this section earns a full band. A judgement, made with reasons — the system tells you what is at stake and leaves the call to you.

Decorative

Free — provided nothing load-bearing lives only there. Covers, dividers, section openers, pull quotes. Go as hard as you like.

The rule above all the others: preserve the brand most strongly in the decorative and narrative surfaces; preserve accessibility most strongly in the layer that carries meaning. The cover can shout. The chart cannot.

02What a component is here

A component is a job, not a shape.

Two things that look similar but do different jobs are two components. Two things that do the same job in different places are one component with a modifier. That is what makes this set survive the move from a deck to a website to a dashboard to an evaluation report without being redrawn — the job travels, the appearance adapts.

03Which container, and why

Containment is where most systems come apart, because a box is a box and people pick one by eye. Here the edge treatment is the instruction to the reader. Choosing a left rule instead of a top rule is choosing to say something different — so the choice is made by asking what you are telling the reader to do, not by what looks balanced.

Read it top to bottom. The first yes wins.

05What a refusal is for

A refusal is not the system being difficult. It is the system declining to make weak evidence look strong.

Every visual decision after rung three is a decision about emphasis — this is the important number, this is the comparison worth making, look here. If the underlying figures cannot support that emphasis, then applying the brand to them is not a neutral act. A confident chart is a claim, and the design is what makes the claim confident. So the honest order is: check the evidence, then dress it. Never the reverse.

There are two strengths of intervention, and they are deliberately different:

Stop

A Fixed rule is broken. No component is produced. This is not a judgement call and there is no override — the fix is to the content, not to the design.

Check

Adaptable. The component is produced, and the system tells you what it noticed and what is at stake. You decide. The reason is recorded either way.
