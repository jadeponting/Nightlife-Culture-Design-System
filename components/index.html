04Containment

Contain with colour and space, not outlines. Before reaching for any of these, check whether you are compensating for a proximity error — you usually are, and the fix is to move things rather than draw a line between them.

Fill

The colour field is the container. The default for accent panels. Needs generous space around it.

Top rule · 9px

White panel, accent bar. Dense pages, tables, long documents where five filled panels would be too much.

Left rule · 12px

Reads as an aside rather than a box. Pull quotes, callouts, asides.

Full-bleed band

Runs edge to edge. Stops being a panel and becomes a change of ground.

Hairline · 1px ink 22%

White panels on chalk, tables, forms. Never for anything carrying meaning.

Flyer register · 2px ink

Covers, posters, dividers. Not the default — so the box means something when it does turn up.

05Breakers

A breaker is a paragraph break for a surface — a separator, not an ornament. One per surface. The mid-statement break is retired: it interrupts the sentence rather than pacing it.

Separator — the default, flush left, between title zone and body

Accent bar — above a pull quote. The only decorative use, and always at the top

Hairline — under a title on dense or document-register surfaces

Stop — under a divider title, with nothing following it

One hero statistic per document. It fails when used twice. Never round to imply precision you do not have — “about 30” is more rigorous than “31” on a modelled estimate. 09Charts

Dot plot, not bar chart, for places. Bars imply a quantity from zero; dots imply a position on a scale. A bar chart with no visible range makes a modelled estimate look like a count. Ranges are plausible intervals, not confidence intervals.

Values sit inside the segments, so the key is a reference rather than a decoder. Four series maximum — there is no fifth ramp colour, and the cap is enforced by the class not existing.

Under protanopia, ramp series 1 and 2 measure 1.10:1 and the lightness order inverts. The ramp survives greyscale, projection, deuteranopia and tritanopia — not protanopia. Wherever both appear together, differentiate by more than colour. The label-at-the-mark rule already covers most cases, which is why it comes first.
